DIY double-sided printed circuit board

Board for a wired headset of a landline phone



TO VIEW THE FULL-SCALE IMAGE, CLICK ON THE PICTURE

Video: test: track widths and gaps of 0.1 mm and less, printer test (resolution 600 and 1200 dpi). The photos present the manufacturing sequence of a DOUBLE-SIDED printed circuit board, using the headset board as an example. Special attention is paid to the alignment of both layers and the quality of etching.
To manufacture a board using the described method, some corrections must be made to the PCB file prepared for ordering boards.
Copy the PCB file and work with the copy, as the changes made will result in a reduction of the diameters of all holes.




Set the diameter to 0.2 mm for all holes on the board, including vias - this will ensure that a small area is etched in the center of the contact pad, allowing the drill to hit the mark even during manual drilling.





We mark the printing boundaries with marker lines (markers) - thin lines that must coincide when aligning the images of both layers of a double-sided board.

Printing will be carried out in two stages, and these same markers during the first printing will show the boundaries of the future print and its size.
In P-CAD, markers can be drawn in the TOP ASSY and BOTTOM ASSY layers using lines with a thickness of 0.1 mm.
Vias with holes to the left and right of the board pattern will also come in handy - if you drill them, it will be clear how to adjust the drill tilt - read about this below.

The markers are drawn in one layer, for example, TOP ASSY.
Since the paper used to obtain the prints has inscriptions and graphics on the reverse side (see the next picture), it is advisable to make several markers in different places, then there will be a better chance that one of them will be outside the zone of inscriptions and it will be better visible through the light when aligning the two prints.

The drawn markers are selected (SELECT), copied with displacement (the selected markers are moved with the mouse while holding down the CTRL key), for example, by one grid step.
Make sure that after this operation  THE SELECTION OF MARKERS IS PRESERVED - do not perform other operations until the actions described below are completed!
Change the layer to BOTTOM ASSY, and in the Edit menu select the Move to Layer command.
Now the SELECTED markers, which ended up  in the BOTTOM ASSY layer, are aligned with the markers in the TOP ASSY layer.

The print of the board image is performed on a laser printer on the backing of a self-adhesive film.
The picture shows the front side of the self-adhesive film and its backing with the inscriptions "333".
The backing has a glossy surface, which allows you to easily separate from it the adhesive surface of the self-adhesive film,
the toner lays down well on the backing; when heating a sandwich of the backing with the print and the fiberglass copper-clad laminate with an iron, the toner adheres to the board foil,
and the backing from the self-adhesive film is easily REMOVED FROM THE BOARD FOIL WITHOUT SOAKING after cooling.

 


Let's start by printing one side of the board. The actions for printing the second side of the board will be the same.

 First, we will print the boundaries on paper, within which the pattern of our board will appear during the next print

 Apply a glue stick to the self-adhesive paper from the backing side along the left and right edges and glue it to the paper so that the markers are covered by the self-adhesive paper

Now separate the self-adhesive paper from the backing with a scalpel and remove  it

Do not touch the glossy surface of the backing with your hands!

Print again on the same paper, but this time with the backing of the self-adhesive paper already glued on.
In the picture at the top: the print of one side of the board still glued to the paper,
at the bottom: the previously printed image of the other side of the board - both images are mirrored relative to the future view of the board itself

To glue the two patterns together, you need to scrape off the gloss along the edges; this can be done with a scalpel. It is important not to touch the pattern at any stage. Therefore, it is convenient to work with a scalpel when the backing is still glued to the paper after printing.
Scrape with the scalpel on the backing away from the pattern so that the debris does not get onto the pattern.
Upon completion of the work, check the cleanliness of the pattern

Apply a glue stick to the cleaned areas and glue them together, trying to align both images.
The glue will not dry instantly, which will allow for a careful alignment using a lens and a light source.

To align both patterns, you will need a lens and a light source, preferably with a narrow radiation pattern - it is convenient to use LED flashlights: they have a bright light, and a centimeter-wide strip of paper with the image of your board will be quite enough to completely cover them and protect your eyes from direct light exposure.
As can be seen in the picture, the LED flashlight is wrapped with an aluminum wire in black insulation, which functions as a stand, allowing you to direct the beam in the right direction.

For the final alignment, it is convenient to use printed markers in the form of thin lines located perpendicularly with a gap.
Additional control - clearance in places where the vias align.

Preparation of copper-clad fiberglass laminate for applying the pattern

In the photo: top - an "envelope" made of glued prints, bottom - the blank. 
For the blank, use a fiberglass laminate with no scratches on the foil. The blank made of copper-clad fiberglass laminate should be about 5 millimeters larger than the pattern - besides the fact that a blank with margins is easier to position under the pattern, scratches appearing along the edge when cutting the blank will not fall into the pattern zone, and burrs can be filed down with a file.
If the blank is cut with metal shears or a guillotine, it may get scratched along the edges and bend (warp), try to flatten it by pressing the corners of the blank against a hard surface.

After flattening, file down the blank, holding the file at an acute angle to the surface - this will additionally smooth the edges of the blank (there will be no raised edges), and the iron will press the pattern better in the center

The blank with processed edges.

To clean the blank, it is convenient to use some kind of household acid-containing gel.
There is no need to clean the blank with sandpaper - small scratches will lead to uneven track edges.

Apply a small drop of gel on the surface of the blank

and rub it over the surface. Do not use cotton wool or cloth for this! Fibers will remain on the ends of the board,  which may get under the pattern. It is better to rub the drop with your hands wearing rubber gloves.

Do not touch the surface of the blank with your hands anymore, hold it by the edges. After rinsing with water, drops may remain on the surface of the blank. The fewer damages and remaining contaminants on the surface, the fewer drops there will be.

the drops can be washed away with a thin stream of water; you need to place the blank so that the stream catches the drop, then slowly withdraw the board from under the stream, directing the board so that the stream eventually ends up on one of the corners of the board. If traces of drops remain, use a roll of toilet paper to remove them - wipe the blank with the edge of the roll. Also apply the edges of the board to the roll.
 Leave the board in a vertical position for final drying - do not insert a board with undried edges into the print.

 to transfer the pattern, you will need a cotton napkin, an iron with a flat sole, and an ironing board will work well as a work surface.
The blank in the envelope of glued prints (it is in the middle in the picture)  is covered with one layer of cloth. The iron is turned on to medium heat (wool) and heats up.
After the iron warms up, press the blank down with it in the envelope covered with the cloth, and hold it still for a few seconds so that the toner adheres to the foil, then you can move the iron over the cloth.
Make sure that the cloth is not shifted by the iron (usually this does not happen).
If you are not sure that the blank is under the iron, you can lift the iron, then place it exactly over the board.
There is no need to move the edge of the iron soleplate over the board.
Just make sure that the iron fits evenly against the surface of the blank during movement, rock it sometimes to feel  that it is pressing down on the entire surface of the blank.
It is enough to roll on the pattern on one side for 50-60 seconds (the temperature adjustment will probably have to be selected experimentally for your iron).
Immediately turn the HOT blank over to the other side and repeat the procedure: the backing of the 333 self-adhesive paper may spontaneously separate from the toner and the blank after cooling.
Let the package of two backings and the blank cool down. After cooling, carefully remove the backings from one, then from the other side of the blank. While removing the backings, check if any toner is left on them; this will help you understand if the pattern was transferred well to the blank.

 The pattern is applied to the blank. We carefully check if there are any defects; for this, it is better to use a lens.

If there are areas where the toner did not adhere, fill them in with a fine permanent marker (1 mm, S or F markers). There are no defects on the blank; to test the marker, we will apply a small spot with it on the blank (in the picture with markers, a spot is applied on the blank above the pattern).








 To hang the blank in the ferric chloride solution, we will drill two holes in the blank at the corners, so that the fishing line threaded through them does not touch the pattern.

Insert one end of the fishing line into both holes from one side - so that the fishing line overlaps the cut edge of the board (see the picture in large scale), then the board will hang without tilting. It is convenient to clamp the fishing line with a clothespin - it will be easier to jerk the board during etching and the fishing line will not fall into the solution.

It is best to etch the board in a water bath in a vertical position, occasionally jerking the board - there is no need to use electric motors or solenoids for jerking: the etching time is 5...20 minutes depending on the quality and temperature of the solution. It is convenient to use transparent containers. For the ferric chloride solution - a flat one from some shampoo, for hot water - made of a 2-liter PET bottle.

containers for boards of different dimensions - allow using a minimum amount of solution. The favorite container is the left one: it fits tightly into the hot water container, does not float and does not tilt, has a large height reserve - the ferric chloride from the board transfers to its walls when you take the board out for inspection or rinsing.

The board is etched in a container with ferric chloride, which is immersed in hot water. Periodically, the board is lifted by the clothespin for control and jerked several times. When carefully pouring hot water, the container is dry outside, usually standing next to the computer at which I work during etching. Since the container is somewhat in the way at the same time - you won't forget about it, as when using a solenoid or motor for jerking, and the board will not over-etch.

 The board after etching. The spot left by the marker for the test above the pattern protected the foil well.

 the centers of the vias and contact pads are well-etched.

Even with careful alignment of the sides, a shift of the via centers is possible.
In the photo - the board from the end; the wire is threaded through the hole - a slight tilt is noticeable.

To make the hole pass through the centers of the contact pads, you can drill the board with a sharpened fragment of a drill bit (in the photo a 0.3 mm drill bit, the length of the working part is about 1.5 mm).
 When passing through the foil, the drill is held vertically, then a tilt is made (the tilt is exaggerated in the picture). Drilling is not carried out completely to the end, then the same is done from the other side of the board, as a result the holes emerge at the center of the contacts on both sides of the board.

Usually, to understand whether it is necessary to tilt the drill and in which direction,  it is sufficient to make a through hole that should be drilled with a larger diameter drill bit (for example, for a terminal or a conductor) - the displacement of the hole on the other side of the board will show how to correct the tilt angle. For large boards, it is better to make several such holes along the edges - uneven stretching of the pattern during printing and toner transfer is possible.

 For the same purpose, along with markers placed along the boundaries of the patterns for aligning the prints, you can place a pair of vias with 0.35 mm holes there as well
